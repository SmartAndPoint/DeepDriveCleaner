DeepDriveCleaner becomes a menu bar app. The command-line tool ships inside it and stays on your `PATH` as `ddc` and `deepdrivecleaner`.

> **DeepDriveCleaner deletes files.** It can remove almost anything it is told to, by you or by an agent acting for you. Use it at your own risk: nobody who made it is responsible for what it removes. Read [DISCLAIMER.md](https://github.com/SmartAndPoint/DeepDriveCleaner/blob/v0.2.0/DISCLAIMER.md).

- **The menu bar app.**
  - A popover shows what the last scan found, by risk class, with the biggest finds and Scan now.
  - The Review window lists every finding by group or by item. You select what to clean, and a confirmation sheet checks each item first and names what would be skipped. It then shows what was deleted, moved to the Trash or skipped, and why.
  - Full Disk Access goes to the app, not to Terminal or the binary.
- **Your own files.** Downloads, Pictures, Movies, Music and iPhone and iPad backups now list what they hold. You can pick files and folders one by one, and they go to the Trash, never deleted. Nothing in them is ever selected for you. Photos and other app libraries, partial downloads, unfinished backups and anything changed in the last 10 minutes are locked.
- **The command line.**
  - `ddc clean --items` cleans an exact list, with `--dry-run` and `--json`; it is how the app cleans.
  - `ddc rules` lists every rule by group.
  - `settings.json` is shared with the app.
  - In a terminal, `ddc` now starts with a short warning that it deletes files and is used at your own risk. It goes to stderr and only when that is a terminal, so `--json` output, pipes, scripts' captured output and the app are unchanged.
  - The README has a full guide to every command.

**Not in 0.2.0, coming in 0.3.0:** Settings beyond Open at login (folders, types, notifications), and the app's own scheduled scans and notifications. Until then scheduled scans stay the CLI's: `ddc schedule install`, as in 0.1.0. The popover's "Scheduled scans off" and the menu's Pause scans are about the app's own scans; an agent from `ddc schedule install` keeps its schedule.

**Upgrading from 0.1.0.**
- `brew upgrade --cask deepdrivecleaner` installs `/Applications/DeepDriveCleaner.app`, and `ddc` now points inside it.
- Grant Full Disk Access to DeepDriveCleaner.app.
- If you use `ddc schedule install`, its scans keep running, but the binary has a new path: add `/Applications/DeepDriveCleaner.app/Contents/Helpers/deepdrivecleaner` to Full Disk Access too, and remove the old Caskroom entry.
- `brew upgrade` quits the app; it opens again at your next login if Open at login is on, or when you open it. Do not upgrade while the app is cleaning.
- The command-line tool is now signed as `com.smartandpoint.deepdrivecleaner.cli`, so the app keeps `com.smartandpoint.deepdrivecleaner` for itself. A manual install from the tarball needs its Full Disk Access grant once more.
- 0.2.0 needs macOS 26 on Apple Silicon. On an older macOS the cask refuses to upgrade and 0.1.0 stays installed; the CLI of 0.2.0 is attached to this release as a tarball for a manual install.
- To go back to 0.1.0: `brew uninstall --cask deepdrivecleaner`, then install the command-line tool from the [0.1.0 release](https://github.com/SmartAndPoint/DeepDriveCleaner/releases/tag/v0.1.0) by hand.

How it was built, decision by decision, is public too: [DeepDriveCleaner-projectstore](https://github.com/SmartAndPoint/DeepDriveCleaner-projectstore).
